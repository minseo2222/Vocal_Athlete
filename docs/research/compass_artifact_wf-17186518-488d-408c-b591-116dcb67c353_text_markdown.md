# Evidence-Graded Review: Classical Voice Pedagogy for Self-Directed Mobile-App Learning

**Evidence scale:** A = strong empirical/clinical (controlled studies, instrumental/physiological data); B = convergence across multiple pedagogical schools; C = partial evidence; D = single school/weak basis; E = anecdotal; F = falsified.

**Scope:** Target learner = a self-directed, unsupervised, healthy adult on a phone, past beginner (48-day) + intermediate core + curve/register application. The app does NOT score performance. On a phone, the only reliably measurable signals are fundamental frequency (F0), timing, and self-report; formant reliability is low. High-risk techniques must be LOCKED until expert sign-off (human-in-the-loop, HITL). Each topic ends with an explicit app-implementability/gating judgment.

## TL;DR
- Of the six topics, only **mobile resonance feedback (Topic 5)** and the **pre-messa readiness markers (Topic 4)** are safely implementable now as non-scored, relative, self-tracked phone tools; **cover/voce chiusa (Topic 1), prescriptive aggiustamento (Topic 2), squillo/singer's-formant induction (Topic 3), and full messa di voce (Topic 4)** must be GATED behind HITL sign-off, because their safety hinges on laryngeal/supraglottic configurations a phone cannot see.
- The **mechanism science is grade A–B** (Sundberg, Titze, Echternach, Henrich/Smith/Wolfe — MRI, EGG, and acoustic studies), but the **prescriptive pedagogy is mostly grade B–D**: schools converge on passive vowel modification and chiaroscuro balance, yet genuinely disagree on active vowel neutralization vs. distinctiveness and on the value of deliberate formant tuning.
- On a phone, **F0, timing, and validated self-report (e.g., the Vocal Fatigue Index) are reliable (A/B)**; **relative spectral measures (CPPS, SPR/LTAS band energy, spectral tilt) are usable within-session and same-device for relative feedback only (B/C)**; **absolute formant tracking is unreliable (grade D for app use)** and must not drive instruction.

## Key Findings
1. **Cover/voce chiusa**: The acoustic mechanism (H2 rising through F1) is grade A; safe gradual acquisition via passive modification + SOVTEs is grade B; unsupervised acquisition without a teacher catching tongue-root depression/laryngeal pressing is grade D — gate it.
2. **Aggiustamento**: Direction of modification is grade B; precise quantitative pitch/degree prescription is grade C and voice-type-dependent; the neutralization-vs-distinctiveness debate is unresolved.
3. **Singer's formant/squillo**: Mechanism is grade A; safe UNSUPERVISED induction at intermediate level is grade D/E — gate it.
4. **Messa di voce entry criteria**: Beyond strain/next-day fatigue, additional objective markers exist (F0 stability under dynamic change, register-transition smoothness, onset/offset control, intensity-envelope linearity, validated fatigue self-report) — grade B/C.
5. **Mobile resonance feedback**: A relative acoustic proxy is feasible (CPPS most robust; SPR/LTAS band energy and spectral tilt as relative indices) — grade B/C; absolute formants unreliable on phones — grade D.
6. **Primary sources**: Real peer-reviewed references identified for every core claim (Topic 6).

## Details

### TOPIC 1 — COVER / VOCE CHIUSA SAFE ACQUISITION

**The mechanism is well established (grade A).** "Cover" in the male upper-middle voice and the female lower passaggio is, acoustically, the point where the second harmonic (2f₀) rises above the first formant (F1). Below it the voice is "open" (voce aperta), with F1 reinforcing 2f₀; above it the voice "turns over" into the closed/covered timbre (voce chiusa). This was demonstrated by Bozeman's acoustic passaggio analysis (Bozeman, "Acoustic passaggio pedagogy for the male voice," *Logopedics Phoniatrics Vocology* 38(2):64–69, 2013) and is underpinned by Titze's source–filter interaction work showing that "lifting" harmonics over F1 is the physical basis of covering (Titze & Worley, *JASA* 126(3):1530–1540, 2009). Real-time MRI in tenors confirms the vocal-tract changes through the passaggio (Echternach et al., *Journal of Voice* 28(3):262–267, 2014).

**There IS a validated, lower-risk progression: passive vowel modification + SOVTEs (grade B).** The convergent, low-risk method is to keep vocal-tract length and shape stable so the turn-over happens *passively* as pitch rises (Bozeman 2013), and to explore the coordination first in semi-occluded vocal tract exercises (SOVTEs: lip trills, tongue trills, straw/flow-resistant-tube phonation, voiced fricatives, humming). SOVTEs have the strongest evidence base in voice training: computer-simulation and inverse-filtering work shows they lower phonation threshold pressure, reduce vocal-fold collision forces, and improve source–filter interaction (Titze, "Voice training and therapy with a semi-occluded vocal tract," *JSLHR* 49(2):448–459, 2006). Clinical efficacy is supported by a randomized controlled trial: Kapsner-Smith, Hunter, Kirkham, Cox & Titze (*JSLHR* 58(3):535–549, 2015) randomized 20 dysphonic subjects (16 women, 4 men) to flow-resistant-tube phonation vs. Vocal Function Exercises vs. delayed controls, and Voice Handicap Index scores improved significantly for both treatment groups relative to the no-treatment group.

**The historical "cover" tradition (lowered larynx) is grade B/D and carries the key risk.** The lowered-larynx "voix sombrée/voce chiusa" descends from Garcia and the 19th-century schools. Voice science supports a *moderately* stable, comfortably low larynx as contributing to the singer's formant and inertive reactance. The DANGER is achieving it by **tongue-root depression / actively pressing the larynx down**, a recognized fault: depressing the tongue root (via the hyoglossus) interferes with laryngeal tilt, produces a dull tone lacking high overtones, and is associated with hyperfunction (Marilee David, "The Retracted Tongue," *Journal of Voice*; corroborated across pedagogy). A depressed larynx is widely described as both incorrect and potentially damaging.

**Warning/stop signals that should halt the learner (grade B/C):** (a) audible cracking, sudden pitch jumps, or register break at the turn — Echternach's tenor perturbation studies show jitter/shimmer rise during register transitions; (b) a tone that gets *duller/darker AND louder* rather than rounder — the hallmark of tongue-root depression; (c) visibly forcing the tongue down or pulling the chin/jaw; (d) throat/jaw/neck tightening, effort spikes, or any pain; (e) next-day fatigue or reduced soft high phonation.

**App implementability: GATE (HITL sign-off required).** F0 and timing can detect the *acoustic* turn-over (H2/F1 crossing) and pitch instability, and the app can teach passive modification and SOVTEs safely. But the app CANNOT see tongue-root depression or laryngeal pressing — the principal injury risk — and formants are unreliable on phones. Therefore SOVTE-based exploration and passive-modification drills can be app-delivered (grade B), but deliberate "covering"/lowered-larynx work should be LOCKED until a teacher confirms the learner is not depressing the larynx.

### TOPIC 2 — AGGIUSTAMENTO QUANTITATIVE PRESCRIPTION

**Acoustic registration / formant tuning by voice type (grade A for the acoustic facts).** The landmark dataset is Henrich, Smith & Wolfe (*JASA* 129(2):1024–1035, 2011), who measured the first two vocal-tract resonances (R1/R2) in 22 classically trained singers by injecting broadband sound at the mouth:
- **Sopranos:** tune R1 ≈ f₀ (R1:f₀) over the upper range, beginning where f₀ rises to meet the vowel's speech R1; many also use R2:2f₀. Per Joliveau, Smith & Wolfe (*JASA* 116(4):2434–2439, 2004) and Henrich et al. (2011), sopranos raise R1 to match f₀ over the higher part of their range, roughly **500 < f₀ < 1000 Hz** (~B4–C6), with R1:f₀ tuning continuing to ~1 kHz for unrounded vowels (/ɑ/, /ɜ/) and R2:2f₀ tuning often occurring simultaneously from about C5 to C6. Lip-rounded vowels (/ɔ/, /u/) fall below the R1=f₀ line near 1 kHz because R1 cannot be raised that far with rounded lips.
- **Altos:** R1:f₀ for low-R1 vowels, switching to R1:2f₀ lower in the range.
- **Tenors/baritones/basses:** R1:2f₀ and R1:3f₀ across parts of the range, with multiple harmonic tunings at lower pitch.

**The "turning over" pitches (grade B/C).** Bozeman's rule (Bozeman, "The Role of the First Formant in Training the Male Singing Voice," *Journal of Singing* 66(3):291–297, 2010): the H2/F1 crossing occurs roughly **one octave below the vowel's F1**; the full "whoop" coupling occurs when f₀ itself reaches F1. Because /i/ and /u/ have the lowest F1, they turn over well below the passaggio (and may need to be actively *opened* to avoid premature closure); mid-close /e/ and /o/ turn over near the primo passaggio; open /ɑ/ near the secondo passaggio.

**Approximate F1 reference values (grade C — speaker/dialect dependent).** Male speech F1 (Peterson & Barney, *JASA* 24(2):175–184, 1952): /i/≈270, /u/≈300, /ɔ/≈570–590, /ɑ/≈730 Hz. In *classical singing*, Titze found F1 modifications cluster most vowels near ~C5 (~523 Hz) in the G4–C5 region (reinforcing f₀), whereas belt raises F1 toward ~C6 (~1046 Hz) to reinforce 2f₀ (Titze, "Formant Frequency Shifts for Classical and Theater Belt Vowel Modification," *Journal of Singing* 67(3):311–312, 2011; Titze & Worley 2009). Female formants run ~3–4 semitones higher.

**Passaggio pitches by voice type (grade B/D — pedagogical convention, individually variable).** Richard Miller's widely cited placements (*The Structure of Singing*, 1986): tenore lirico primo ~D4 / secondo ~G4; lyric baritone ~B3 / E4; bass ~A3 / D4; soprano primo ~Eb4–G4 / secondo ~Eb5–F#5; mezzo ~E4–F4 / ~E5–F5; contralto ~G4/Ab4 region. These are approximate and disputed (Stark notes Miller cited no published literature for the male two-passaggi/zona model).

**Direction and degree of modification (grade B for direction; C/D for degree).** The convergent rule is Miller's aggiustamento ladder: as pitch ascends through the zona di passaggio, vowels migrate toward neighbors — [i]→[ɪ]→[e]→[ɛ]→[ɑ]→[ɔ]→[o]→[ʊ]→[u] — front vowels raising R1, back vowels lowering R1, toward a more central configuration; rounding (arrotondamento/copertura) intensifies at and above the secondo passaggio. Miller frames this as graduated copertura, explicitly distinct from abrupt heavy German "Deckung." Titze's general rules (lengthening/rounding lower all formants; jaw opening raises F1; tongue fronting/arching lowers F1 and raises F2) provide the mechanism (summarized in Nix, "Vowel Modification Revisited," NCVS).

**The scholarly debate: neutralization/centralization vs. distinctiveness (genuinely unresolved — both sides presented).**
- *Pro-modification/neutralization:* Miller's school and Coffin's chromatic vowel chart prescribe systematic migration/substitution toward neutral vowels for resonance balance.
- *Minimize active modification:* Bozeman argues that once singers understand passive modification and maintain a settled, neutral laryngopharynx, *heavy active* modification (e.g., singing /i/ as /y/) becomes "reduced or unnecessary," and that lip-rounding unnecessarily reduces high-frequency energy and distorts the vowel (Bozeman, *Journal of Singing*, 2017; *Kinesthetic Voice Pedagogy*, 2017).
- *Empirical caution on deliberate tuning:* Sundberg, Lã & Gill (*Journal of Voice* 27(3):278–288, 2013) found that exact formant–harmonic coincidence is NOT clearly favorable for all male singers — "some authors claim that such coincidence is favorable… whereas other authors have found evidence for advising against it." Source–filter coupling can also *destabilize* vibration when a strong harmonic crosses a formant (Titze 2008; Titze et al. 2008). So vowel distinctiveness is partly preserved in practice, and over-tuning has costs.

**App implementability: GATE for prescriptive degree; PARTIAL for awareness.** The acoustic *facts* (which harmonic to favor, roughly where turn-over occurs by voice type and vowel) can be presented as educational content and the app can use F0 to locate where a learner is in their passaggio. But because (a) reliable formant tracking is unavailable on phones and (b) the correct *degree* of modification is individual and contested, an unsupervised app must NOT prescribe a specific vowel-shape target as if scored. Awareness/exploration content = grade B (safe); targeted formant-tuning prescription = GATE.

### TOPIC 3 — SINGER'S FORMANT / SQUILLO SAFE INDUCTION

**Mechanism (grade A).** The singer's formant cluster (~2.5–3.5 kHz, a clustering of F3–F5) arises when the epilarynx tube is narrowed relative to a widened pharynx. Sundberg's classic model (Sundberg, "Articulatory interpretation of the 'singing formant,'" *JASA* 55(4):838–844, 1974) requires that the pharynx cross-sectional area be "at least six times" that of the larynx-tube outlet, plus a widened sinus Morgagni and wide piriform sinuses — conditions fulfilled when the larynx is lowered. (Caveat: in-vivo MRI has not always confirmed the 6:1 ratio — e.g., later imaging finds smaller ratios — and Mainka et al. emphasize hypopharyngeal expansion; the acoustic model and the anatomical findings are best treated as complementary.) Titze & Story (*JASA* 101(4):2234–2243, 1997) model the epilarynx as a brass-mouthpiece-like impedance matcher. The aryepiglottic narrowing that produces "ring"/twang was first videolaryngoscopically documented by Yanagisawa, Estill et al. ("The contribution of aryepiglottic constriction to 'ringing' voice quality," *Journal of Voice* 3(4):342–350, 1989); recent MRI confirms oropharyngeal and aryepiglottic narrowing for twang ("Oropharyngeal and Aryepiglottic Narrowing for Twang: An MRI Study," *Journal of Voice*, 2024).

**Twang/aryepiglottic narrowing is NOT inherently hyperfunctional (grade B).** Aryepiglottic constriction occurs in everyday speech and is distinguishable from false-vocal-fold (ventricular) hyperfunction, which is the thing to avoid (clinical consensus; Lombard & Steinhauer 2007 used twang therapeutically for hypophonic voice). So "ring" can in principle be induced without pressing.

**BUT safe UNSUPERVISED induction at intermediate level is grade D/E.** There is no controlled evidence that a self-directed learner can reliably induce squillo without sliding into ventricular constriction or raised subglottal pressure, and distinguishing healthy aryepiglottic narrowing from harmful false-fold constriction requires laryngoscopic or expert auditory confirmation. The safest, evidence-supported on-ramp is again SOVTEs and resonant-voice work that biases toward efficiency rather than force (Titze 2006; resonant voice: Verdolini Abbott). Deliberate "squeeze for ring" is the failure mode.

**App implementability: GATE (HITL).** A phone cannot distinguish healthy epilaryngeal narrowing from false-fold hyperfunction. The app may give *relative* feedback that energy in the 2–4 kHz band increased (see Topic 5) and may deliver SOVTE/resonant-voice drills (grade B), but explicit squillo "induction" instruction should be LOCKED until a teacher signs off, because the principal risk (ventricular hyperfunction) is invisible to the device.

### TOPIC 4 — MESSA DI VOCE OBJECTIVE ENTRY CRITERIA

**What messa di voce demands (grade A on the physiology).** Full messa di voce — a slow pianissimo→fortissimo→pianissimo on a sustained pitch — requires continuous, coordinated change of subglottal pressure and glottal adduction while pitch is held constant. High-speed videolaryngoscopy + EGG studies show that slower execution is harder for stability and that it stresses the crescendo/decrescendo symmetry of open quotient and adduction ("Influence of Messa di Voce speed on vocal stability" — separate untrained-subject and professionally-trained-singer studies, *PLOS ONE*, both HSV+EGG; earlier loudness-envelope work by Titze and colleagues).

**Objective, self-measurable readiness markers BEYOND strain/next-day fatigue (grade B/C):**
1. **F0 stability under dynamic change** — the learner can crescendo and decrescendo on a sustained pitch WITHOUT pitch drift or jumps. This is phone-measurable (F0 is the most reliable phone signal). A pre-messa swell showing pitch sag at high SPL or pitch lift at low SPL is not yet ready.
2. **Onset/offset control and pianissimo viability** — reliable balanced (flow) onsets and offsets without glottal slamming or breathy escape, and the ability to sustain a true pianissimo without register flip. The decrescendo-to-pianissimo half is the harder one (it shows greater perturbation in the studies).
3. **Register-transition smoothness** — no audible register break across the dynamic sweep (perturbation rises at transitions per Echternach), since messa di voce can cross registration thresholds as intensity changes.
4. **Timing/evenness** — a linear, monotonic SPL ramp over a target duration (e.g., even ~3-second phases), measurable as smoothness of the intensity envelope.
5. **Validated fatigue/effort self-report** — a low, stable Vocal Fatigue Index (Nanjundeswaran, Jacobson, Gartner-Schmidt & Verdolini Abbott, *Journal of Voice* 29(4):433–440, 2015), with no post-task elevation of perceived effort. That study reported "excellent" discrimination (AUC 0.91, ~90% sensitivity and specificity per factor), with proposed cutoffs of Factor 1 ≥24, Factor 2 ≥7, Factor 3 ≤7. Phonation threshold pressure is the research gold-standard fatigue marker (Titze) but is not phone-measurable.

**App implementability: PARTIAL — pre-messa monitoring SAFE; full messa di voce GATE.** The app can reliably track F0 stability and intensity-envelope smoothness on a phone and can administer the VFI as structured self-report (grade B); these serve as objective readiness gates. But progressing a learner into *full* fortissimo messa di voce unsupervised risks pressed phonation at the loud peak that the app cannot see; full messa di voce should require HITL sign-off, with the pre-messa metrics above used as the documented entry criteria.

### TOPIC 5 — MOBILE-PHONE CLASSICAL RESONANCE FEEDBACK

**Bottom line: a NON-scored, RELATIVE, same-session, same-device proxy is feasible; absolute measures and formants are not.**

**Phone-mic reliability (grade A on the measurement question):**
- **F0 is reliable on phones** across multiple validation studies (Awan et al., *JSLHR* 67(6):1712–1730, 2024, found correlations with a reference GRAS microphone typically r > .90).
- **CPP/CPPS is among the most robust** acoustic measures and is relatively task- and recording-robust, though it shows systematic *bias* between devices (so use within-device, relative; Awan et al. 2024; Murton, Hillman & Mehta, *AJSLP* 29(3):1596–1607, 2020; the 2018 ASHA expert panel recommended CPP over jitter/shimmer).
- **Jitter and shimmer are NOT reliable on phones** (problematic random error; Titze's ~5% reliability ceiling for perturbation) — do not use for feedback.
- **Formant frequencies are unreliable on phones** for fine resonance tuning — non-flat frequency response, automatic gain control, and codec processing distort the spectral envelope. Grade D for app-based absolute formant tuning.

**Candidate resonance proxies and their robustness:**
- **Singer's-formant band energy (2–4 kHz) / Singing Power Ratio (SPR):** SPR = peak amplitude 2–4 kHz minus peak 0–2 kHz. Omori, Kacker, Carroll, Riley & Blaugrund (*Journal of Voice* 10(3):228–235, 1996) computed SPR on sung /a/ in 37 singers and 20 nonsingers: SPR of sung /a/ was significantly greater in singers than nonsingers, and within singers significantly greater for sung than spoken /a/, with SPR correlating with perceived "ringing." Usable as a RELATIVE within-session index, but absolute values depend on mic response, so cross-device comparison is invalid (grade B/C).
- **LTAS / spectral tilt-slope:** LTAS reliably differentiates trained classical singers in the 2–4 kHz region across studies and shows good within-singer test–retest reliability (Johnson & Kempster; Oliveira Barrichelo et al.; Ferguson et al.). Spectral tilt/slope is a stable relative indicator of "ring" vs. "dull." Task-dependent, so compare like-for-like tasks (grade B/C).
- **Spectral centroid:** a simple, robust brightness proxy that rises with added high-frequency energy. Reasonable for relative feedback (grade C — mechanically sound, less specifically validated for classical "ring").
- **CPPS:** robust, but it indexes periodicity/clarity (and breathiness) more than resonance per se; best used as an efficiency/quality companion rather than a ring meter (grade B).
- **Harmonics-to-noise ratio:** less robust on phones; secondary at best.

**App implementability: SAFE as relative, non-scored feedback.** The app should compute, within a single session on the same device, a relative "ring/brightness" index from 2–4 kHz band energy or spectral tilt, plus CPPS for clarity, and show the learner *changes* (this attempt brighter/clearer than the last) rather than scores or absolute targets. This aligns with the no-scoring constraint and the low formant reliability. Explicitly warn against cross-device or cross-session absolute comparison.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Ship now (safe, app-implementable):**
- Deliver SOVTE and passive-modification exploration drills (relevant to Topics 1 and 3) framed as efficiency/coordination work — not as "covering" or "ring induction."
- Build the **relative resonance feedback** module (Topic 5): within-session, same-device 2–4 kHz band-energy / spectral-tilt "ring index" + CPPS "clarity index," shown as change over attempts; never scored, never cross-device.
- Use **F0 stability + intensity-envelope smoothness + VFI** as objective pre-messa readiness dashboards (Topic 4).
- Present the acoustic *facts* of registration/aggiustamento (Topic 2) as education, anchored to the learner's own measured passaggio (derived from F0).

**Stage 2 — Gate behind HITL sign-off (unlock per-skill after a qualified teacher verifies, via video or live check, the absence of tongue-root depression and ventricular hyperfunction):**
- Deliberate "cover"/lowered-larynx work (Topic 1), explicit squillo induction (Topic 3), full fortissimo messa di voce (Topic 4), and any prescriptive vowel-target / formant-tuning instruction (Topic 2).

**Thresholds/benchmarks that should change the staging:**
- If validated on-phone formant estimation achieves acceptable error under real recording conditions, parts of Topic 2 could move from GATE to in-app.
- Auto-relock advanced modules and prompt rest/teacher contact if: VFI rises toward the Factor-1 ≥24 cutoff; soft-high-phonation ability drops; or F0 instability/register breaks appear at the turn-over.
- Any pain, persistent hoarseness beyond ~24–48 h, or cracking at the passaggio = immediate stop + referral.

## Caveats
- Mechanism science (grade A) governs safety and is robust; the *prescriptive* pedagogy is mostly grade B–D and individually variable — the app should teach principles and self-monitoring, not enforce single-school targets.
- The neutralization-vs-distinctiveness debate (Topic 2) is genuinely unresolved; deliberate exact formant–harmonic tuning is NOT unambiguously beneficial and can destabilize phonation (Sundberg et al. 2013; Titze 2008).
- The Sundberg 6:1 pharynx:epilarynx ratio (Topic 3) is a model value not always confirmed in vivo; treat the acoustic model and MRI anatomy as complementary, not identical.
- Passaggio pitch tables and speech F1 values are conventional approximations, not measured constants; F1 values are speaker- and dialect-dependent.
- All phone spectral measures are valid only as RELATIVE, within-session, same-device indices; absolute values and formants are unreliable, and SPR/CPPS show device bias.
- The central safety gap for unsupervised app use is that the two principal injury mechanisms — tongue-root/laryngeal depression and ventricular (false-fold) hyperfunction — are INVISIBLE to a phone, which is why high-risk techniques must remain gated.
