## Topic 1 — CCM vs Classical Voice-Injury Epidemiology

### New peer-reviewed sources

1. **Bretl, M., Boyer, J., Lerner, M., Smith, B., Lobo, R., Zhang, Y., et al. (2023). "Vocal Fold Pathologies Among Undergraduate Singing Students In Three Different Genres," *The Laryngoscope* 133(7).** — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/lary.30533
   *Longitudinal follow-up: prevalence rose to 22% (classical) / 39% (musical theatre) / 27% (CCM) by Year 3. Single most important new dataset on genre-by-year trajectory.*

2. **Childs, L. F., Anderson, A., Anciano, A., Vandenberg, S. (2023). "Association of Genre of Singing and Phonotraumatic Vocal Fold Lesions in Singers," *The Laryngoscope* 133(11).** — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/lary.30414 ; PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36196907/
   *Country/folk, gospel/jazz, MT singers more likely to present with phonotraumatic lesions; opera singers higher proportion of pseudocysts.*

3. **Pestana, P. M., Vaz-Freitas, S., Manso, M. C. (2017). "Prevalence of Voice Disorders in Singers: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28342677/
   *Pooled 46.09% voice-disorder prevalence among singers across all genres — anchor meta-analytic prevalence figure.*

4. **Sielska-Badurek, E. M. et al. (2024). "Population characteristics and self-assessment of speaking and singing voice in Polish CCM singers — exploratory cross-sectional study," *Frontiers in Public Health.*** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11133608/
   *22% of Polish CCM students started training already with vocal nodules; large untreated burden in CCM.*

5. **Behlau, M. et al. (2021). "Independence of Vocal Load From Vocal Pathology Across Singing Genres," *J Voice.*** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199721000084
   *Vocal load alone does not predict pathology; technique and hygiene mediate. Important nuance for "more practice = more injury" assumption.*

6. **Lechien, J. R. et al. (2021). "Laryngeal Pathologies Associated with the Genre of Singing and Professional Singing Status in a Treatment-Seeking Population," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33270237/
   *Pop singers 63.2% pathology prevalence in treatment-seeking cohorts; full-time pros 60.8%, part-time 62.2%, amateurs 45.1%.*

7. **Pawełczyk, M. et al. (2022). "Considerations and demands in the voice care of contemporary commercial singers in occupational health and safety aspects," *Med Pr.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35133326/
   *Frames CCM voice as occupational health / OSHA category; argues CCM rarely seeks help until severe.*

8. **Toles, L. E. et al. (2025). "Vocal Fold Kinematics in Phonotrauma From High Speed Videoendoscopy," *The Laryngoscope.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40709447/
   *2025 high-speed-video kinematic data on the actual phonotrauma mechanism — collision speed/closure asymmetry. Mechanism-level evidence.*

9. **Galindo, G. E. et al. (2023). "Effect of nodule size and stiffness on phonation threshold and collision pressures in a synthetic hemilaryngeal model."** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36732229/
   *Computational/synthetic model showing self-amplifying loop: nodule → ↑collision pressure → ↑hyperfunction → ↑nodule.*

10. **Nayak, S. et al. (2025). "Worldwide Prevalence of Voice Disorders Among Schoolteachers: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis," *J Voice.*** — https://www.jvoice.org/article/S0892-1997(25)00171-7/abstract
    *Newest 2025 meta-analytic teacher-prevalence figures — replaces older estimates.*

11. **Caretti, A. et al. (2025). "Voice Disorders in Teachers: Risk Factors and Impact on Quality of Life," *J Voice.*** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0892199725001766
    *Newest cross-sectional QoL impact data; teacher cohort.*

12. **Schwarz, K. et al. (2025). "Protective Factors for Vocal Health in Teachers: The Role of Singing, Voice Training, and Self-Efficacy," *Int J Environ Res Public Health* 22(7):1018.** — https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/22/7/1018
    *Cross-sectional, n=124. Singing background and prior voice training were independent protective factors. Direct evidence for prevention pedagogy.*

13. **Ohlsson, A.-C. et al. (2016). "Voice Disorders in Teacher Students — A Prospective Study and a Randomized Controlled Trial," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26474714/
    *Prospective + RCT in teacher trainees: voice training reduced incidence. Rare RCT-level evidence on prevention.*

14. **Park, K. & Behlau, M. (2018). "Voice Disorders and Voice Knowledge in Choir Singers," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30104126/
    *Choir singer prevalence + knowledge gap; useful amateur baseline.*

15. **Devadas, U. et al. (2021). "A survey of vocal health in church choir singers," *Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol.*** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8266785/
    *84% of church choir singers reported ≥2 vocal symptoms; striking amateur prevalence.*

16. **Pestana, P. M. et al. (2023). "Amateur choir singers — Does good vocal health matter?," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36908211/

17. **Salturk, Z. et al. (2017). "Prevalence of Hearing Loss in Teachers of Singing and Voice Students," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27839986/
    *51.7% voice-teacher hearing-loss rate — secondary occupational risk under-discussed.*

18. **Devadas, U. et al. (2021). "Prevalence of Laryngopharyngeal Reflux Symptoms, Dysphonia, and Vocal Tract Discomfort in Amateur Choir Singers," *J Voice.*** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34404581/

19. **Dietrich, M. & Verdolini Abbott, K. (2022). "Relationships Among Personality, Daily Speaking Voice Use, and Phonotrauma in Adult Female Singers," *J Voice.*** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9150681/
    *Personality and speaking-voice load mediate phonotrauma — supports speaking-voice as injury vector for singers.*

20. **Gunjawate, D. R. et al. (2024). "Vocal Health Awareness in Undergraduate Singing Students," ETSU dissertation.** — https://dc.etsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4866&context=etd
    *Quantifies awareness gap in collegiate singers — argues for curricular intervention.*

### Remaining gaps
- No long-term cohort tracking the same singers from training entry → 10-year career; trajectories beyond Year 3 are inferred.
- Ethnic/regional diversity is poor — almost no East Asian or African cohort epidemiology in CCM.
- Causal chain *bad-technique → injury* is mechanistically modeled but rarely tested in human RCTs (ethical limits).
- Genre boundaries (rock vs pop vs R&B vs metal) are collapsed in most studies — sub-genre injury rates absent.

### Unexpected discoveries
- **The Bretl 2023 longitudinal flip:** Classical students had 0% pathology Year 1 but 22% by Year 3 — classical is *not* injury-free, just delayed-onset; the Year-1 dataset everyone cites is misleading.
- **Galindo 2023 self-amplifying nodule mechanism** (collision pressure rises with nodule size) is a quantitative mechanism that can ground curriculum claims.
- **Behlau 2021 "Independence of Vocal Load From Vocal Pathology"** — challenges the assumption that more singing = more injury. Technique > volume.

    *Action-research curricular framework — alternative to brand pedagogies.*

12. **Vala, B. (2021). "Mixed Voice for the Bel Canto and Musical Theatre Singer," *UNT dissertation.*** — https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1833442/m2/1/high_res_d/VALA-DISSERTATION-2021.pdf

    *RCT-level SOVT-protocol comparison directly relevant to CVT/EVT warm-up claims.*

15. **Chen, T. et al. (2024). "Short-Term Effects of Semi-Occluded Vocal Tract Therapy on the Phonation of Children With Vocal Fold Nodules: An RCT," *JSLHR.*** — https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2024_JSLHR-24-00243

### Remaining gaps
- **Zero true Phase III RCTs** on CVT, EVT, or SLS as branded methods vs control. Everything is feasibility, exploratory, or non-randomized.
- No method-vs-method head-to-head trial (e.g., CVT vs EVT vs LMRVT).
- No long-term (≥2-year) outcome follow-up published for any branded method.
- "Critical" literature on EVT/CVT in academic form is thin; most critiques are dissertations or trade-press essays, not peer-reviewed.

### Unexpected discoveries
- **Bartlett & Naismith 2020:** Veteran CCM pedagogues *don't* identify with any single named method — undermines the consumer-marketing premise of method certifications.
- **CVT4MTD (NCT05365126)** — there *is* an active registered clinical trial pipeline; the field is finally institutionalizing CVT testing.
- **Steinhauer & Klimek 2024 (RTSS mapping)** — a serious response to the "EVT isn't clinical" critique that the field hasn't yet absorbed.

### Existing-tool tech-stack notes (non-academic, for differentiation)
- **Yousician / Riyaz / SingSharp / Vanido / SingTrue:** marketed as "AI" but tech-stack is largely classical pitch-detection (autocorrelation/YIN) + rule-based scoring; few have published validation. Sources: Singing Carrots ([link](https://singingcarrots.com/blog/top-7-ai-vocal-coaches/)), RichlyAI ([link](https://richlyai.com/blog/voice-training-app/)), SingTrue ([link](https://www.musical-u.com/apps/singtrue/)).
- **VoceVista / Sing&See:** desktop spectrogram tools used in academic pedagogy; long-standing. ([link](https://www.vocevista.com/en/), [link](https://www.singandsee.com/forteachers))

### Remaining gaps
- No *singer-specific* CAPE-V/GRBAS automation — current models trained on clinical pathological speech, not stylistic singing.
- No published technique-classification AI (e.g., "is this belt vs mix vs head?"). The closest is Hutchins/Larrouy-Maestri CVT-mode work referenced in COMP1.
- Almost no validation on *Korean-language* singers / K-pop styles.
- Wearable dosimetry (PNAS 2023) is academic-only; no consumer product yet.

### Unexpected discoveries
- **PNAS 2023 wearable vocal dosimeter** — top-tier journal coverage of voice-load wearable; usually missed in pedagogy reviews.
- **Manfredi 2017 + 2022 validation studies** show consumer-app frequency measurements are mostly accurate — but jitter/shimmer/AVQI degrade quickly off-clinical-grade microphones. Important for product positioning.
- **VoiceMOS 2024 Track 2 architecture (Pitch-and-Spectrum-Aware)** — moves beyond MFCC features to pitch-conditioned spectral modelling; current SOTA reference for any new app.

### Remaining gaps
- Korea has very few cohort or RCT-level studies on singing pedagogy; most domestic work is single-author conceptual / case-study / pedagogy-design.
